A short history of the web

The browsers MiniChrome comes after, in the order they came:

1990, WorldWideWeb
Tim Berners-Lee's first browser, at CERN, on a NeXT computer. It was an editor too: a page could be changed in place, and a link made by pointing at another page.
1991, the Line Mode Browser
Nicola Pellow's browser for any terminal: the page as text, and a number after each link, to type.
1993, NCSA Mosaic
Marc Andreessen and Eric Bina's browser: pictures in the page, among the words, and one click to follow a link.
1994, Netscape Navigator
Made by much of Mosaic's team: the page shown while it loads, and soon in colour, with tables and pictures the text flows around (its extensions).
1995, JavaScript, and Internet Explorer
Brendan Eich's language, in Netscape 2: a page becomes a program. Microsoft's browser, from Mosaic's code, comes with Windows: the browser war.
1996, style sheets
Håkon Wium Lie and Bert Bos's CSS: the looks of a page out of its tags, in rules.
1998, Mozilla; 2004, Firefox
Netscape gives its source away. What is rebuilt from it, years later, is Firefox: tabs, and tools to look inside a page.
2003, Safari
Apple's browser, its engine WebKit grown from KDE's KHTML.
2008, Google Chrome
On WebKit at first: a process a tab, a JavaScript compiled to machine code (V8), one field for addresses and searches, and pages that are whole applications. Its web is the one MiniChrome is written for.

Each age has its page here, written as its time wrote them: 1993 (HTML alone), 1995 (Netscape's tags), 1996 (style sheets), pages that are programs, and today's.

The first web page's address was http://info.cern.ch/hypertext/WWW/TheProject.html and it still answers: the first web page is still served by CERN, and the Line Mode Browser can be tried again in a simulator.
